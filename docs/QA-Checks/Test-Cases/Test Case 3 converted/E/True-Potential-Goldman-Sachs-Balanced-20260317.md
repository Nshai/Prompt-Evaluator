## February Update

Global equities delivered further gains in February as value style shares significantly outperformed their growth style peers. Emerging Market (EM) equities markedly outpaced their developed market counterparts despite weakness in China. In developed markets, Japanese shares led the gains, while US equities proved a laggard. In fixed income, long-dated UK Gilts outperformed amid lower yields. Elsewhere, gold delivered solid gains.

The True Potential Goldman Sachs Balanced Fund returned 2.11% (net of fees), with the Fund's sizeable allocation to equities proving beneficial in a strong month for the asset class. Less positively, a large allocation to US equities weighed on returns. The Fund's allocations helped performance overall, with the tilt towards US and European healthcare and US quality style shares performing well. The tilts towards Mexican, Japanese and EM shares also provided a positive contribution, as did the tilt towards European blue-chip companies. However, the tilt towards US technology detracted, as did the tilt towards Indian and South African equities. In fixed income, long-dated US Treasuries and UK Gilts outperformed, while EM debt also did well. Meanwhile, a sizeable exposure to corporate bonds via the Goldman Sachs Global Credit Portfolio was also supportive. Elsewhere, the Fund's exposure to assets via the Passive Global Infrastructure ETF portfolio delivered the best gains overall, while exposure to alternatives via the Goldman Sachs Global Equity Volatility Fund and the Goldman Sachs Alternative Trend Portfolio was also beneficial.
