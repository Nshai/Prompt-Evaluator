## How your money is looked after

The People's Pension is governed by an independent corporate trustee, The People's Pension Trustee Ltd, which has responsibility for running the scheme and for looking after all the pots of money held by the scheme. The scheme is set up under a trust structure - this is intended to keep the scheme's assets separate from your employer and so provides you with a level of security. The trustee ensures that The People's Pension is run in the best interests of its members and other beneficiaries, and in accordance with the Rules of the Scheme and the law. ## Ways you can take your money

The options available to you with The People's Pension depend on how much is in your pension pot when you choose to access it.

If your pension pot is worth £10,000 or less when you access it, you may be able to take your money all in one go as a 'small pot lump sum'.

With occupational pension pots (like The People's Pension), you can take as many small pot lump sums as you want. The first 25% is usually tax free, with the other 75% being subject to tax. Depending on the size of your pot and your tax status, parts of it may be subject to higher rates of tax.

Before  you're  able  to  take  your  money,  all  contributions into your pension pot must have stopped. You must also meet all HMRC's rules about when a small pot lump sum can be taken.

If your pension pot is worth more than £10,000 when you access it, you can either take your money as a lump sum, or take your money a bit at a time in one of two ways.

1. The first option is through 'flexi access drawdown' (FAD). You take your tax-free cash up front (usually up to 25%) either a bit at a time or in one lump sum. For every £1 you take as tax-free cash, £3 will be moved into a flexi-access drawdown account. Then each time you take money out of the flexi-access drawdown account, you may pay tax on the full amount of each lump sum. It's added to your taxable income and, depending on your tax status, parts of it may be liable for higher rates of tax.
2. The second option is through an 'uncrystallised funds pension lump sum' (UFPLS). This involves spreading your tax-free cash across all withdrawals. Each time you take money from your pension pot, 25% of it is tax free. The other 75% is added to your taxable income. Depending on your tax status, parts of it may be liable for higher rates of tax.

People's Partnership doesn't offer an option to purchase an annuity, which offers a guaranteed income, therefore there are no guarantees to the amount you may be able to take as income from your savings. But you can use your money to buy an annuity, or to access any of the other options listed above, from another provider.
