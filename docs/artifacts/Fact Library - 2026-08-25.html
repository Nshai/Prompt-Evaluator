Advice Evidence POC · golden source

Fact Library

The 44 published fact definitions the system currently extracts and verifies from a suitability report — the canonical library, generated directly from the seeded golden source. Every claim the pipeline produces is an instance of one of these facts, validated against the definition's value type, modality allowlist, and constraints.

Reading a card: the chips show the value's type and how many instances a case may carry; “may be stated as” lists the accepted modalities (actual = true now · reported = client-stated · intended = client plans it · assumed = adviser/modelling assumption · projected = future value from an illustration · recommended = part of the advice · conditional = only if a condition applies · unknown = indeterminable). Consistency-reconciled facts additionally take part in the SR-internal repeated-values check. “Extraction detail” expands the working guidance and signal phrases.

Client identity

client.identity · v1
composite one-per-subject

Identity attributes the report states for a named person: full name, date of birth, and National Insurance number where present. One claim per person (client, spouse or partner, dependent). This is the anchor for identity-consistency checking: supporting documents should agree on who the case is about, and mismatched or differently-spelled identities across documents are a review signal, not a formatting nuance.

May be stated as: reported · actual · unknown  ·  About: client, spouse, dependent

Extraction detail

Extraction guidance: Extract the person's full name exactly as spelled, the date of birth verbatim where stated, and the National Insurance number where present. Produce one claim per named person, bound to that person as the subject. Do not derive age from the date of birth (stated ages are person.age claims), do not normalise name spellings or date formats, and do not merge two differently-named people into one identity even when the context implies they are the same person.

Signals: full name, date of birth, DOB, National Insurance number, NI number, client details

Income source and amount

income.source · v3
composite many consistency-reconciled

A stated income amount for the client or spouse from an identified source (employment, State Pension, other), with its frequency. Underpins affordability and capacity-for-loss reasoning.

May be stated as: reported · actual · projected · assumed · unknown  ·  About: client, spouse, partner

Extraction detail

Value parts: amount (money), amountMin (money), amountMax (money), frequency (string), source (string), net (boolean)

Extraction guidance: Extract each income amount as a separate claim: earner, source, amount, frequency (per week/month/annum), and whether net. An income stated as a range ('drawing approximately £3,000 to £3,500 monthly') uses amountMin/amountMax — preserve the range. A future income (e.g. a State Pension payable from a future age) is modality 'projected'; expected future self-employment earnings are also 'projected'. Preserve the amount and frequency exactly as stated ('every four weeks' is not 'per month'); do not normalize periods. An income that has ceased or will cease (e.g. business drawings ending at retirement) is still a claim; record the cessation in the statement, and extract expected-but-unquantified income (e.g. final business distributions pending accounts) with the statement even without an amount. Defined benefit scheme pensions in payment are source 'defined-benefit-pension'. A partner's stated contribution towards household bills is an income claim with source 'partner-contribution'. Where both gross and net figures are stated for the same income, extract each figure as stated. Income currently in payment stated as a present fact (for example 'you are in receipt of your full State Pension') is modality 'actual' or 'reported'.

Signals: earning, income, State Pension, per week, per month, per annum, net, gross per annum, defined benefit pension, pro-rata, contributes, self-employed earnings, drawing from the business, every four weeks
